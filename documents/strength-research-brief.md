# Strength and Conditioning for Endurance Cyclists: Research Brief

Source material for three prescriptive reference files of the cycling-plan-coach skill.
Athlete profile: 39 y male, 91 kg, 10 y cycling, ~1.5 y on/off lifting (currently "new/none recent", reports 50 kg squat and deadlift), tight quads and lower back, full gym. Target: Badlands Ultra (~750 km, self-supported, heavy climbing, heat, 12+ h/day). Season Base -> Deload -> Build -> Deload -> Refine (taper), 30+ weeks, strength 1-2x/week. Limiter: low power on steep climbs and at high intensity.

## How to read this brief

**Evidence grades used throughout**
- **(A) Strong**: RCTs or systematic reviews/meta-analyses in cyclists or closely related populations. Even so, the cycling literature is small (mostly n = 10-30 per trial) and the newest meta-analysis rates certainty as LOW across all outcomes.
- **(B) Practitioner consensus**: widely used, biologically plausible, mostly extrapolated from general resistance-training science or coaching practice.
- **(C) Speculation / extrapolation**: reasoned but no direct evidence for this population.

**Verification tags.** `[V]` = I opened the abstract or full text (or a search-result abstract) in this session. `[U]` = cited from background knowledge and NOT re-verified in this session; check before quoting in user-facing text. Several PMC pages returned CAPTCHA pages, so details for those are limited to search snippets and are flagged. No citation below was invented; where authors/details could not be confirmed I say so.

**A structural caveat that runs through everything:** no trial studied strength training in the context of a 700+ km, multi-day self-supported event. Everything about "durability over 12 h/day" is extrapolation from 3-h protocols (Area 5).

## 1. Does strength training improve cycling performance?

### 1.1 Summary of the evidence

**Meta-analysis (A, but low certainty).** Llanos-Lagos, Ramirez-Campillo & Sáez de Villarreal (2025, *Eur J Appl Physiol*) pooled 17 studies (262 participants, 60 women; mean VO2max 61 mL/kg/min; mean PEDro 5.53/7). Heavy strength training (HST) effects (Hedges g) `[V]`:
- Cycling performance (TT / all-out tests): g = 0.46 (moderate), p = 0.016
- Anaerobic power (Wingate/sprint): g = 0.56 (moderate), p = 0.024
- Cycling efficiency: g ~0.35 (small), p = 0.012
- No significant effect on VO2max (g = -0.04), power at VO2max (0.16), MMSS (0.07) or anaerobic capacity (0.24)
- Mean intervention: 13.6 wk (5-25), 2.2 sessions/wk (1-3), ~84% 1RM (67-95%), ~3.5 sets/exercise, ~4 exercises. Most common exercises: half squat (12 studies), single-leg leg press (10), single-leg hip flexion (8).
- No significant moderators (sex, frequency, duration, baseline fitness) - but that reflects low power, not proof of no difference.
- Interpretation: gains come via non-aerobic pathways (neuromuscular/efficiency/fibre-type), not VO2max.

**Older reviews (A/B).** Rønnestad & Mujika (2014, *Scand J Med Sci Sports*) concluded there is "the most compelling evidence of an additive effect" of heavy strength training to endurance training in cycling, and recommended heavy strength for cycling economy `[V]`. Yamamoto et al. (2008, *J Strength Cond Res*) reviewed running (RE +4.6%; 2.9% performance in 3-5 km); the same group later reviewed cycling (Yamamoto et al. 2010, title verified via ResearchGate listing only) `[V, details U]`. Beattie et al. (2014, *Sports Med*) reviewed strength training in endurance athletes generally (economy, vVO2max, power) `[V title/abstract]`. Berryman et al. (2018, *IJSPP*) meta-analysis was in running/middle-long distance (RE d = 0.63 when >24 sessions; trivial when <24) - relevant only as a "dose needs to be chronic" signal `[V]`.

### 1.2 Key individual trials (all well-trained cyclists unless stated)

| Study | Design | Protocol | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rønnestad, Hansen & Raastad 2010, *Eur J Appl Physiol* 108:965 `[V]` | 20 well-trained cyclists, 12 wk | 4 lower-body lifts, 3 x 4-10RM, 2x/wk | Increased 1RM, thigh CSA, performance (40-min all-out), retained with 1x/wk for ~2 wk (per abstract) |
| Rønnestad, Hansen & Raastad 2010, *Eur J Appl Physiol* 110:1269 `[V]` | 12 wk 2x/wk then 13 wk 1x/wk in-season vs endurance only | As above | Maintenance 1x/wk preserved CSA and strength gained and further improved performance determinants |
| Aagaard et al. 2011, *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 21:e298 `[V]` | 14 young elite cyclists, 16 wk | Concurrent heavy strength | 45-min TT capacity improved; type IIX -> IIA fibre shift; MVC and RFD up; capillarisation unchanged |
| Sunde et al. 2010, *J Strength Cond Res* `[V]` | 8 wk, 3x/wk | Half squats 4 x 4RM | 1RM +14%, RFD +17%, cycling economy +4.8%, work efficiency +4.7%, TTE at pre-Wmax +17% |
| Vikmoen et al. 2016, *Scand J Med Sci Sports* `[V]` | 19 well-trained women, 11 wk | Heavy strength 2x/wk | 40-min all-out power up; better fractional utilisation of VO2max and economy; correlated with quad CSA (r = 0.73) and IIAX-IIX decline (r = -0.63) |
| Rønnestad et al. 2015, *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 25:e89 `[V]` | 16 young elite cyclists, 25 wk | Heavy strength (2x/wk initial 10 wk) | Wingate peak power, Wmax, power at 4 mmol lactate and 40-min mean power up in strength group only |
| Rønnestad 2022, *Front Sports Act Living* case report `[V]` | Elite cyclists across 1.5-3.5 seasons | See Area 2 | Leg press force +22%, power +31%, 6-s sprint power +22%; plateau in years 3-4 |
| Kristoffersen et al. 2019, *Front Physiol* 10:1132 `[V]` | 28 cyclists, 4 wk prep + 6 wk | HST vs short-sprint training (SST) | SST better for all sprint outcomes; HST better for 1RM (+9.3%); 5-min all-out similar (+3.3% HST vs +5.7% SST, no significant difference); VO2max no difference |
| Bishop et al. 1999 (via Rønnestad & Mujika 2014 and Vikmoen 2017 text) `[V, secondhand]` | 12 wk, squats 2-8RM | Trained female cyclists | No change in endurance performance - the main null study |
| "On-bike vs off-bike" RCT, 2025, *PMC12244400* `[V snippet only; authors/journal not verified]` | 37 well-trained cyclists, 10 wk | Full squat vs all-out high-intensity pedalling efforts | No significant differences between groups in any parameter; off-bike group "tended to increase injury-related symptoms" |

### 1.3 Mechanisms (B-A)
- IIX -> IIA fibre shift, greater quad CSA (Aagaard 2011; Vikmoen 2016).
- Lower relative effort per pedal stroke at a given power (more force reserve -> delayed recruitment of less efficient type II fibres) - proposed in Rønnestad & Mujika (2014) `[V]`. Not directly measured; treat as (B).
- Tendon stiffness / neuromuscular efficiency - proposed, evidence indirect (C).
- Sprint power gains can be large in strength-naive riders but plateau across seasons (Rønnestad 2022).

### 1.4 Effect-size reading for this athlete
For a strength-naive athlete the meta-analytic averages (g ~0.35-0.56) understate the likely *early* gains and overstate what an already-strong athlete will get. Most trials used cyclists with no strength background (Rønnestad 2012: "no strength training experience"), so the athlete's "new/none recent" status is the same population that benefited.

### Prescriptive takeaways (Area 1)
- Include heavy (>=80% 1RM equivalent) strength work 1-2x/week; expect benefits mainly in (a) sub-maximal efficiency, (b) sprint/peak power, (c) 5-45 min all-out power, not VO2max. **Confidence: medium-high** (multiple RCTs; low-certainty meta-analysis; small samples).
- Do not promise FTP or VO2max gains from lifting. **Confidence: high.**
- Sprint/short-burst power is a fair *secondary* target: HST +1-2% vs SST +5-6% for 6-s sprint - so for the "steep climbs / high intensity" limiter add short max-effort on-bike sprints or low-cadence torque efforts, not just lifting. **Confidence: medium** (single RCT, n=28).
- Treat gym strength as a supplement to, not a substitute for, on-bike specific work; a 2025 RCT found on-bike high-intensity pedalling efforts matched squats (snippet-level verification). **Confidence: low-medium.**

## 2. Loading parameters

### 2.1 What worked in successful protocols
The Rønnestad/Vikmoen/Kristoffersen group protocols are remarkably consistent `[V]`:
- **Exercises:** half squat (Smith machine), one-legged leg press (one foot at a time), standing one-legged hip flexion, toe raise/ankle plantar flexion. (Meta-analysis confirms half squat, single-leg leg press, single-leg hip flexion as the most common.)
- **Sets/reps/load:** 3 sets per exercise; alternating sessions at ~10/8/6RM and 6/5/4RM. Example (Vikmoen 2017): wk 1-3: 10RM and 6RM sessions; wk 4-6: 8RM and 5RM; wk 7-11: 6RM and 4RM. Kristoffersen 2019: 3 x 8RM/5RM then 6RM/4RM. Meta-analytic mean ~84% 1RM.
- **Rest:** ~2 min between sets/exercises.
- **Tempo/intent:** "maximal acceleration during the concentric phase, while the eccentric phase was performed more slowly" (Rønnestad 2022).
- **Frequency:** 2x/wk development for 10-12 (up to 25) weeks; then 1 session per 7-10 days maintenance.
- **Session volume:** 4 exercises x 3 sets = ~12 hard sets, plus warm-up. Meta-analysis mean ~14 sets/session.
- **Progression:** repetition-maximum based ("systematic variation between 4 and 12RMs") - load increased when the target reps were achieved. Note "xRM" means the load you can lift x times; whether every set was taken to failure is not consistently reported (unclear).
- Sunde 2010: 4 x 4RM half squat 3x/wk, 8 wk (fewer exercises, heavy, high frequency) also worked.

### 2.2 Heavy vs hypertrophy vs high-rep vs power
- **Heavy, low-rep, high-intent** has the strongest cycling-specific evidence (A/B) `[V]`.
- **High-rep, endurance-style leg work** has weak/absent evidence for improving cycling performance; the null study (Bishop 1999) used heavy squats to failure but only 12 wk with low frequency, so the "null" is not cleanly attributable to load. Rønnestad & Mujika (2014) note heavy loads (roughly 4-10RM) are what showed effects (also Koninckx et al. 2010 cited secondhand) `[V secondhand]`. Confidence: medium.
- **Hypertrophy** (8-12 reps) is not directly tested against heavy in cyclists; Vikmoen and Rønnestad both found performance change correlated with quad CSA gain, so some hypertrophy is probably helpful during base (B/C).
- **Power/plyometric/short sprints:** SST > HST for sprint outcomes in cyclists (Kristoffersen 2019 `[V]`). Plyometric-specific cycling trials were not found in this search (gap).
- **Volume-response general science:** more weekly sets produce more hypertrophy (Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017, *J Sports Sci* `[V]`), but concurrent endurance training blunts response and time is limited; low-volume/high-intensity is the endurance-athlete norm (B).
- **Rest intervals:** >2 min needed to maximise strength gains in resistance-trained; 60-120 s adequate in untrained (Grgic et al. 2018, *Sports Med* `[V]`). Use 2-3 min on heavy compound sets.

### 2.3 Intensity autoregulation
- RIR-based RPE scale (Helms et al. 2016, *Strength Cond J* `[V]`): RPE 10 = 0 RIR, 9 = 1 RIR, 8 = 2 RIR, 7 = 3 RIR. Load-velocity approaches are a (B) alternative (not researched in detail here).
- For an endurance athlete carrying high aerobic load, practitioner consensus (B) is to keep most heavy sets at 1-3 RIR rather than failure, to limit soreness (Kristoffersen 2018: CK and soreness elevated up to ~45 h after heavy strength `[V]`).

### Prescriptive takeaways (Area 2)
- Core prescription for trained athletes: **3 exercises x 3 sets, 4-6 reps at ~85-90% 1RM (RIR 1-3), 2-3 min rest, 2x/wk**, concentric as fast as possible, eccentric ~2-3 s. **Confidence: medium-high** (mirrors trial designs).
- Rep range alternates: 8-10 reps (75-80%) in general preparation; 3-6 reps (85-90%) in max-strength. **Confidence: medium.**
- Weekly hard sets on lower body: 6-10 sets (novice/base start) -> 9-15 sets (max). **Confidence: medium (trial mean ~14 sets/session, but time-poor amateur likely needs less).**
- Fast concentric intent in every rep. **Confidence: medium** (universally used in protocols; not isolated as a variable).
- Avoid endurance-style 15-25 rep leg circuits as the main driver. **Confidence: medium.**

## 3. Exercise selection

### 3.1 Pedalling mechanics
- Single-joint hip and knee extensors (gluteus maximus, vasti) are the main power generators through the downstroke (approx. top dead centre to ~90-145 degrees); hamstrings and gastrocnemius/soleus act mostly to transfer/stabilise force through the lower half; plantarflexors/tibialis co-activate to stabilise the ankle (EMG literature: Ericson 1986 *Acta Orthop Scand* `[V snippet]`; Ryan & Gregor 1992 `[V snippet]`; intramuscular EMG study, *Eur J Appl Physiol* 2016, `[V title]`). Hip flexors assist the recovery/upstroke.
- Steep-climb riding raises crank torque and hip-extension contribution and reduces cadence, so strong hip and knee extensors have plausible relevance (C; not directly tested).
- Joint-angle specificity: cycling operates at knee angles ~40-110 degrees flexion; half squat (to ~90 degrees knee) matches the range better than deep squats. Evidence for angle-specific adaptation exists in general strength science but was not specifically verified for cyclists (B).

### 3.2 Recommended lifts (evidence-informed)
1. **Half/parallel squat or leg press** (knee-hip extension, the studied staple; A/B). For this athlete with tight quads/back: **leg press, goblet squat, box squat, safety-bar or trap-bar (hex-bar) squat-deadlift hybrids** allow an upright torso.
2. **Single-leg leg press / split squat / step-up (moderate box height, ~knee level)**: the studied unilateral staple; also address left-right asymmetry (A for leg press; B for step-up).
3. **Hip hinge (trap-bar deadlift, Romanian deadlift, hip thrust/glute bridge)**: not in the classic protocols (which omitted hinges) but standard S&C practice for glute/hamstring and lower-back robustness (B). Trap-bar deadlift permits more upright posture and less lumbar shear than a straight-bar deadlift (B; biomechanical rationale).
4. **Single-leg hip flexion (cable or ankle-weight, standing)**: in 8 of 17 meta-analysed studies; targets the pull-up phase (A/B).
5. **Calf raise (straight and bent knee)**: "toe raise" is in every Rønnestad protocol (A/B for inclusion). Ankle stiffness/stability rationale is (C).
6. **Trunk**: loaded carries, Pallof press, dead bug, side plank, back extension/Sorensen-type endurance holds (B; Area 5).
7. **Upper body / posture**: horizontal row, lat pulldown, face pull/rear delt, push-up or dumbbell press, overhead carry (B; Area 5). Masters cyclists surveyed used more upper-body work when >=50 y (Vikestad & Dalen 2024 `[V]`).

### 3.3 Injury-specific substitutions for THIS athlete (tight quads, lower back)
- **Tight/stiff quads or poor knee flexion tolerance**: use leg press with box/limited depth (knee ~90 degrees), Bulgarian split squat with front foot elevated only if pain-free, reverse sled drag/backward walking, and 2-3 min couch-stretch-style quad lengthening or foam roll AFTER sessions (not before heavy lifts). Avoid deep ATG squats and knee-dominant volume spikes in early weeks (B; tendon adaptation lags muscle).
- **Lower back tightness**: prefer trap-bar deadlift, hip thrust, leg press, split squat, chest-supported row and back extension (45 degrees) over barbell back squat and straight-bar deadlift in the first block; maintain neutral spine and bracing; use RIR >=3 on hinge patterns. Add posterior-chain endurance (Sorensen holds 3 x 30-60 s, Bird-dog) (B).
- **Pain rules**: stop or regress a lift if pain >3/10 that persists >24 h or alters mechanics; keep hinging load submaximal until no pain for 2 consecutive sessions (B; standard clinical-practice heuristic, not cycling-specific).

### Prescriptive takeaways (Area 3)
- Each strength session: one knee-dominant bilateral (leg press / trap-bar squat / goblet squat), one unilateral (step-up / split squat / single-leg leg press), one hinge (trap-bar deadlift / RDL / hip thrust), one calf raise, plus 1-2 trunk and 1 pull movement. **Confidence: medium** (studied core = squat + single-leg press + hip flexion + calf; hinge/trunk/pull are consensus).
- For this athlete start with trap bar and leg press rather than straight-bar back squat/deadlift. **Confidence: medium (B).**
- Include single-leg hip flexion 3 x 6-10 each side in max-strength blocks. **Confidence: medium (A-B).**
- Keep hip thrust loads moderate/high-rep (3 x 8-12) for glute endurance; no direct cycling trial evidence. **Confidence: low.**

## 4. Periodization across the season

### 4.1 What the trials did
Rønnestad's protocols timed strength to the **preparatory (winter) period** (Nov-April) at 2x/wk, then **1 session per 7-10 days during competition** (April-Sept) - and reported that this maintained gains and further improved performance `[V]`. In the 2010 RCT, maintenance of strength and CSA persisted with 1x/wk for ~13 weeks and cycling performance still improved. Rønnestad (2022) case report: leg-press force +22% after 1.5 yr, plateauing in years 3-4.
Survey data (Vikestad & Dalen 2024, 555 male master cyclists >=35 y) `[V]`: ~79-82% strength-trained off/pre-season (avg ~2 sessions/wk) declining to ~60% (1.2 sessions/wk) in race season; barriers: fatigue/soreness (51%), time (36%).

### 4.2 Detraining and retention
- Training cessation reduces muscular performance overall; effect larger in older (>65) and inactive; small in recreational athletes (Bosquet et al. 2013, *Scand J Med Sci Sports* `[V abstract]`). Specific "~3 weeks before measurable loss" timing is from background knowledge `[U]`.
- Minimal dose for maintenance: Bickel et al. 2011 (*Med Sci Sports Exerc*) - one-third of the training dose better preserved fibre-type shifts; older adults needed a higher dose than young adults to maintain hypertrophy (strength retained better) `[V]`. Spiering et al. 2021 (*J Strength Cond Res*): intensity is the key variable; frequency and volume can be reduced substantially (endurance VO2max maintained up to 15 wk with ~66% volume cut) `[V]`. Strength maintenance figures beyond this (e.g., "1 session/wk for 32 wk") are `[U]`.
- Practical inference: heavy loads matter more than volume for keeping strength (B).

### 4.3 Interference effect
- Wilson et al. 2012 (*J Strength Cond Res*, 21 studies, 422 effect sizes) `[V]`: interference depends on modality, frequency, duration of endurance work; **running, not cycling, blunted strength/hypertrophy**; aerobic gains not compromised.
- Rønnestad et al. 2012 (*Eur J Appl Physiol*) `[V]`: in well-trained cyclists, high-volume endurance training attenuated hypertrophy/1RM/squat jump/RFD gains from 12 wk of strength (S alone > S+E), though S+E still improved.
- Schumann et al. 2022 (*Sports Med*, 43 studies) `[V]`: concurrent training did not significantly reduce maximal strength or hypertrophy vs strength alone but reduced explosive-strength gains.
- Order/timing: a 2024 review of 15 studies (Vikestad & Dalen, *Sports* 12:226 `[V]`) found small, non-conclusive sequence effects for endurance outcomes; 11/15 no difference; 3/15 favoured endurance first when the endurance session was high-intensity. Participants mostly untrained/moderately trained.
- Recovery: Kristoffersen et al. 2018 (*Front Physiol* 9:155 `[V]`) - after a heavy strength session (well-trained cyclists, n=11) power (CMJ, sprint) recovered within 23-47 h, but CK/myoglobin remained elevated to ~45 h and soreness peaked at ~45 h. Suggests >=24 h before a quality bike session (their inference).

### 4.4 Scheduling heuristics (B unless stated)
- Prefer strength on **easy/endurance-ride days or rest days**, at least 24 h (ideally 36-48 h for the first weeks or after a heavy hinge session) before threshold/VO2/climb-repeat sessions.
- If same-day: hard bike session in the morning, strength >=3-6 h later (protect quality of the interval session), and keep the day's second session low-cardio. Avoid strength immediately before key intervals.
- Never heavy lower-body work the day before key sessions, long rides, or events (B; supported by soreness kinetics).
- Deload weeks in the plan: reduce strength by ~40-50% volume, keep the load (see intensity as the retention driver) (B).
- Do NOT perform heavy leg strength in the final ~5-7 days pre-race; last heavy session ~10-14 days out; short, low-volume (1-2 sets, submaximal, RIR >=3) neural primer optional ~5-7 days out (B/C; no cycling taper-strength RCT found).

### 4.5 Taper
Taper evidence is for endurance load, not strength: Bosquet et al. (2007, *Med Sci Sports Exerc* `[V]`) - 2-week taper with exponential 41-60% volume reduction, maintaining intensity and frequency was most effective; endurance athletes' largest effects at 8-14 days. There is no direct RCT on strength training in a taper for cyclists (gap). The "Refine" phase ending in a taper should apply the same principle to strength (reduce volume ~50%, keep a small amount of heavy, no fatiguing eccentrics).

### Prescriptive takeaways (Area 4)
- **Base**: 2x/wk, general prep for 3-4 wk (anatomical adaptation) then max-strength 6-8 wk. **Confidence: medium.**
- **Build**: 1-2x/wk; 2x if endurance load allows, otherwise 1 heavy session + 1 short power/unilateral session. **Confidence: medium.**
- **Refine**: 1x/wk heavy maintenance (2-3 exercises, 2-3 sets, 3-5 reps at 85-90%, RIR 2-3), stop ~10-14 days before event. **Confidence: medium (1x/wk maintenance = A/B; stop timing = C).**
- **Deload weeks**: ~50% volume, keep load, RIR 3-4. **Confidence: medium (B).**
- **Taper**: 0-1 sessions in last 14 days; nothing heavy in last 5-7 days. **Confidence: low-medium.**
- Separate strength from hard bike by >=24 h; strength on easy/rest days. **Confidence: medium.**

## 5. Ultra-endurance / bikepacking specificity

### 5.1 Durability
Durability = onset and magnitude of deterioration of physiological performance over prolonged exercise (Maunder et al. 2021, *Sports Med* `[V]`). Direct evidence that strength training helps it:
- Vikmoen et al. 2017 (*Physiol Rep*) `[V]`: 11 wk heavy strength (3 sets, 10RM -> 4RM, 4 exercises, 2x/wk) in well-trained women; after **180 min at 44% Wmax**, 5-min all-out power +7.0 +/- 4.5% (strength + endurance) vs +3.3 +/- 7.1% (endurance only), d = 0.62; VO2 and HR in the final 2 h of the prolonged ride were lower.
- Rønnestad, Hansen & Raastad 2011 (*Scand J Med Sci Sports*), 5-min all-out performance after 185 min at 44% pVO2max, improved with strength `[V title/snippet only; details U]`.
- Mechanism: lower relative effort per pedal stroke and/or delayed type II recruitment prevents the drift in efficiency (proposed; C).
- **Extrapolation to 12+ h/day for several days is speculative (C).** Neither muscular endurance nor injury data exist for events of this length.

### 5.2 Muscular endurance vs max strength
No evidence that high-rep "muscular endurance" gym work beats heavy work for ultra performance. The mechanistic argument is that raising the ceiling (max strength) makes sub-maximal submaximal efforts a lower fraction of capacity. Practitioner view: keep a *small* amount of higher-rep unilateral/trunk endurance work in base. (B/C)

### 5.3 Trunk, lower back and posture
- Low back pain is the most common musculoskeletal complaint in cyclists; neck and back pain up to ~60% (search-result summary of injury reviews `[V snippet]`). Cyclists with LBP show core muscle activation imbalances, back-extensor endurance deficits and increased lumbar flexion (Streisfeld et al. 2017, *Sports Health* `[V title]`; details from search summary).
- A systematic review of bike-fit interventions for cyclist LBP exists (PMC12907770, `[V title only]`) - bike position is a first-line lever.
- Exercise interventions improve lumbar-extensor endurance (Sorensen test) in non-specific LBP populations (meta-analysis, *Sci Rep* 2021 `[V snippet]`) - this is general LBP data, not cyclist-specific.
- A trial comparing conventional resistance training vs core exercises on road-cycling power exists (PMC11137631; results not retrieved) `[V title only]`.
- Prolonged cycling alters lumbar flexion-relaxation (PMC13075085 `[V title only]`), consistent with lumbar tissue load accruing over hours (B).
- Upper-extremity: ulnar neuropathy (handlebar palsy) is the most common cycling nerve injury, esp. after multi-day/ultra rides; ~1/3 of cycling overuse injuries involve the hands; prevention = bike fit, hand-position changes, padding, avoid sustained loading (search summaries; PubMed 27886836, 34798999 `[V]`). Strength value here is (C); position changes matter more.

### Prescriptive takeaways (Area 5)
- Include 5-10 min trunk/posture work every strength session: anti-extension/rotation (dead bug, Pallof, side plank 3 x 30-45 s), back-extensor endurance (Sorensen/45-degree extension 2-3 x 30-60 s), loaded carry 2-3 x 20-30 m. **Confidence: medium (B) for benefit; LBP evidence is not cyclist-specific.**
- Upper body: 2 sets each of row, push-up/press, face pull/rear delt, 8-12 reps. **Confidence: low-medium (B/C).**
- Direct durability evidence: heavy strength improved late-ride power after 3 h; do not overclaim for 12+ h. **Confidence: medium for 3 h; low for ultra.**
- Bike fit and hand/position variability are more important than gym work for numbness and back pain. **Confidence: medium.**

## 6. Warm-up and session structure

### 6.1 RAMP framework
Jeffreys' RAMP (Raise, Activate, Mobilise, Potentiate; UKSCA 2006 / Prof. Strength & Cond. 2007) `[V via search summaries; original not opened]`:
- Raise: 3-5 min low-intensity bike/rower/walk to raise temperature and HR.
- Activate and mobilise: dynamic drills targeting the session's muscles and joints (glute bridges, bodyweight squats, hip-flexor lunges with reach, thoracic rotations, ankle rocks, cat-camel).
- Potentiate: progressive rehearsal of the session's lifts with increasing load/intent.

Evidence for RAMP as a superior structure is limited to team-sport studies (e.g., PMC12234454, youth soccer); for cyclists it is (B).

### 6.2 Ramp-up sets for the first heavy lift (B)
Reference the day's top-set load T:
- Set 1: 8-10 reps @ empty bar/light (~30-40% T)
- Set 2: 5 reps @ 50% T
- Set 3: 3 reps @ 65-70% T
- Set 4: 2 reps @ 80-85% T
- Set 5 (optional if T >= 85% 1RM): 1 rep @ 90% T
Rest 60-120 s between ramp sets, 2-3 min before the first working set. Total ramp ~5-8 min. Subsequent lifts need 1-2 ramp sets. For a beginner on RIR-based sets, 3 ramp sets suffice.
Volume of warm-up sets should not fatigue: keep ramp reps low and fast.

### 6.3 Stretching
Behm et al. 2016 (*Appl Physiol Nutr Metab*) `[V]`: immediate performance change after static stretching -3.7%, dynamic +1.3%, PNF -4.4%; static stretches held >=60 s per muscle showed larger deficits (-4.6%) than <60 s (-1.1%). So: dynamic before lifting; static or foam-rolling after the session or on separate days for range-of-motion goals.

### 6.4 Cool-down and mobility for cyclists (B)
- 5 min easy spin/walk; then 5-8 min static/PNF or loaded stretches with ~30-60 s per position: hip flexors (couch stretch, half-kneeling), quads (prone), hamstrings (supine strap), thoracic extension over bench/foam roller, calves/ankle dorsiflexion (knee-to-wall), pigeon/figure-4 for glutes.
- Mobility 5-10 min on off days, particularly hip flexor and thoracic work; keeps hip angle and torso comfortable in aero/drop position. No cyclist-specific trials on mobility routines were found (gap).

### Prescriptive takeaways (Area 6)
- Warm-up 10-15 min total: 3-5 min Raise, 5 min Activate/Mobilise, 5-8 min ramp sets. **Confidence: medium (B).**
- Ramp: 40%x8, 50%x5, 65%x3, 80%x2, 90%x1 of top-set load. **Confidence: medium (B; concrete steps are convention).**
- Static stretch <=30-45 s, post-session only. **Confidence: medium-high.**
- Total session length 45-60 min including warm-up. **Confidence: medium.**

## 7. Masters / older athletes (35-50)

Direct evidence is sparse, and a 39-y-old is at the young end of "masters".
- Louis et al. 2012 (*Eur J Appl Physiol*) `[V snippet]`: 3 weeks, 3 sessions/wk of 10 x 10 knee extensions at 70% 1RM (3 min rest) improved delta efficiency in masters endurance athletes (mean 51.5 y); a young group was also tested (interpretation details not verified).
- Bickel et al. 2011 `[V]`: in 60-75 y, higher weekly dose is needed to maintain hypertrophy than in 20-35 y; specific strength was preserved.
- Bosquet 2013 `[V]`: strength loss after cessation greater in >65 y; no evidence of concern at 39.
- Survey (Vikestad & Dalen 2024) `[V]`: older cyclists prioritise bone health (45% of >=50 vs 33% younger); main barrier is fatigue (51%).
- A 2023 concurrent-training meta-analysis in adults 50-73 y reports improved strength, power and cardiorespiratory endurance (search snippet; authors not verified) `[V snippet, weak]`.
- Whether masters need more or fewer sets is **not resolved**: general aging science suggests slightly slower recovery and reduced anabolic sensitivity (B/U), which argues for maintaining intensity (load) but not volume, and longer inter-session recovery. Tendon adaptation lags muscle in everyone; masters athletes may benefit from slow progression in new tendon-loading exercises (B).
- Recovery at 39 with a 12-15 h/week cycling load: soreness/fatigue are the main practical problem (Kristoffersen 2018; Vikestad & Dalen 2024).

### Prescriptive takeaways (Area 7)
- 39 y does not require special modification beyond sensible load management: keep sets moderate (6-12 hard lower-body sets/session), RIR 2-3 early, add 24-48 h separation. **Confidence: medium.**
- Emphasise warm-up quality, tempo control on eccentrics, and sleep 7-9 h. **Confidence: low-medium (B).**
- Add bone-loading (heavy carries, impact-lite skipping/box step-downs, deadlift pattern), because cycling is not bone-loading. **Confidence: medium** (general bone health; cyclist-specific evidence is survey/rationale-level).

## 8. Novice / returning lifters

### 8.1 Ramp and block structure
General principles (B; supported by the fact that all cycling trials began in strength-naive cyclists):
- Weeks 1-3: technique and tissue tolerance: 2 x 8-12 at RIR 3-4 (RPE 6-7), tempo 3-1-1.
- Weeks 4-6: 3 x 6-8 at RIR 2-3.
- Weeks 7-12: 3 x 4-6 at RIR 1-2 (~80-88% 1RM). Compare Vikmoen 2017's 11-wk ramp from 10RM to 4RM.
- Technique gates before adding load: bracing and neutral spine under load; hinge pattern without lumbar rounding; squat/leg-press to target depth without knee valgus; single-leg step-up with control for 8 reps/leg at bodyweight or light load; 2 consecutive sessions with pain <=2/10.

### 8.2 Starting loads
- Reported 50 kg squat/deadlift at 91 kg is ~0.55 x BW - low for a 10-year cyclist with lifting exposure, and could reflect conservative reporting, poor recent practice, or old data. Treat as a *provisional* 3-5RM-equivalent and verify by the first-session build-up (RIR method).
- Estimating 1RM from reps: Epley 1RM = w x (1 + reps/30) `[U]` (accurate to ~+/-5-10% at <=8 reps); RPE/RIR-based charts (Helms 2016 `[V]`).
- Suggested first-session working loads (B/C): goblet squat 16-24 kg x 10; trap-bar deadlift 40-50 kg x 8; leg press 60-80 kg x 10; step-up bodyweight to 2 x 8-10 kg x 8/leg; RDL 30-40 kg x 10. These are conservative anchors, to be adjusted by RIR.
- Strength standards: commonly cited practitioner targets for endurance athletes are ~1.0-1.5 x BW deadlift/trap-bar and ~0.75-1.25 x BW squat/leg-press-equivalent after 6-12 months, but I did NOT verify a citable source for cyclist-specific relative-strength standards (`[U]`; treat as low-confidence heuristic). Rønnestad's trials report absolute gains (+16-22% leg press force after the first winter) rather than benchmark ratios.

### 8.3 Progression
- Double progression (B): pick a rep range (e.g., 6-8); add load (typically +2.5-5 kg upper, +5-10 kg lower on leg press/trap bar, ~+2.5 kg on squat/step-ups) when all sets reach the top of the range at the target RIR.
- Weekly load increments for a novice: ~+2.5-5% per week for the first 4-6 weeks (practitioner; rate slows as tendons/technique catch up). Neural gains dominate early strength (B) - expect ~15-25% strength increase in 8-12 weeks (Rønnestad's 2 sessions/wk gave ~+20% leg strength at 10 wk `[V snippet]`; Sunde +14% in 8 wk `[V]`).
- Time to working loads: ~3-4 weeks to reach a useful working load (~70% eventual 1RM); ~10-12 weeks to reach 4-6RM zone.

### Prescriptive takeaways (Area 8)
- First 3 weeks are anatomical adaptation: 2 x 8-12, RIR 3-4, **no failure sets**. **Confidence: medium.**
- Progress by double progression; increase load only when all sets hit the top of the rep range at RIR >=2. **Confidence: medium.**
- Plan to reach 3 x 4-6 at RIR 1-2 by ~week 8-12. **Confidence: medium.**
- Treat the reported 50 kg squat/deadlift as a provisional anchor; confirm in session 1. **Confidence: high (practical).**
- Do not use published relative-strength standards for cyclists as hard gates. **Confidence: high that they are not established.**

## 9. Autoregulation and monitoring

### 9.1 Tools
- RIR-based RPE (Helms 2016 `[V]`): quick, cheap, learned within a few weeks; less accurate for beginners (B, general literature `[U]`; expect early error and biased under-reaching).
- Velocity-based training: valid but requires equipment; optional (B).
- Session RPE and soreness rating 0-10 (B). Readiness inputs elsewhere in the skill (WHOOP/HRV) are modulators, not gates (C).

### 9.2 Rules (B; derived from recovery kinetics of Kristoffersen 2018 and consensus)
- **Too easy** (RIR >=4 at end of all sets): add load next session (+5% lower body).
- **On target** (RIR 1-3): repeat load, add a rep, or add load per double progression.
- **Too hard** (RIR 0 or form breakdown or sets missed): repeat or reduce the load by 5-10% next session.
- **Failed one set:** keep load, reduce next session's first set by ~5%.
- **Failed two consecutive sessions on the same lift:** cut load 10% and rebuild, or swap variation.
- **Skipped 1 session:** resume the plan without catching up. **Skipped >=2 weeks:** re-enter at ~85-90% of last loads at RIR 3 for one week. (Consistent with detraining data: performance loss small in short breaks; Bosquet 2013.)
- **Soreness:** DOMS >=6/10 at 48 h -> reduce lower-body volume ~30% next session and keep away from hard bike days; soreness that persists >72 h or comes with joint pain -> deload and reassess.
- **Deload triggers:** end of a 3-4 wk block (planned), persistent fatigue/HRV decline, two "too hard" sessions in a row, or increased knee/back symptoms.
- **Failure sets:** avoid to failure on compound lifts in-season; occasional RIR 0 is not necessary for endurance athletes (B).

### Prescriptive takeaways (Area 9)
- Adjust by RIR after each main lift: <4 RIR -> hold/progress; 0 RIR/missed -> back off 5-10%. **Confidence: medium (B).**
- Soreness rule: >=6/10 at 48 h -> cut volume 30%. **Confidence: low-medium.**
- Always tell the athlete what change was made and why (aligned with repo convention).

## 10. Myths and pitfalls

| Myth / pitfall | Evidence-based response | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Lifting will make me heavy / hurt power-to-weight." | Cycling trials show performance gains with small-to-moderate lean-mass changes (Rønnestad 2010; Vikmoen 2016 show quad CSA rises and correlate with better performance). Body mass at 91 kg is more a nutrition/aerobic-fitness question; a caloric surplus is not part of the protocol. Hypertrophy is bounded by high endurance volume (Rønnestad 2012). | Medium (A/B) |
| "High reps for endurance." | Heavy loads show the effects (Area 2); high-rep leg work lacks support. | Medium |
| "Lifting the day before a key session is fine." | Muscle-damage markers and soreness remain elevated ~45 h post heavy lifting though power recovers (Kristoffersen 2018). | Medium |
| "Strength training raises VO2max/FTP." | Meta-analysis: no effect on VO2max or power at threshold; effects on efficiency, sprint, all-out. | High |
| "Interference effect makes lifting pointless with high cycling volume." | Cycling shows minimal interference (Wilson 2012); hypertrophy is attenuated at high volume (Rønnestad 2012) but performance benefits remain. | Medium-high |
| "Stop lifting in season." | 1x/wk maintenance preserved strength and improved performance (Rønnestad 2010). Survey data show most amateurs do reduce (Vikestad & Dalen 2024). | Medium-high |
| "Lifting right before the event/in the taper is helpful." | No supporting evidence; risk of soreness. | Low (thin evidence) |
| "Squat deep for full range." | No evidence of extra cycling transfer; may aggravate tight quads/knee. | Low-medium |
| "Long stretches before lifting prevent injury." | Static stretching >=60 s reduced immediate performance (Behm 2016); no clear injury-prevention effect. | Medium |
| "Core = planks only." | LBP research supports back-extensor endurance and control; specific cyclist trials limited. | Low-medium |

## Decision table for the coach skill

Notation: sets x reps @ intensity (RIR). "2x" = frequency per week. Exercise emphasis uses the six categories from Area 3: **K** knee-dominant bilateral, **U** unilateral, **H** hinge, **C** calf, **T** trunk, **P** pull/upper. Intensity in %1RM is approximate; use RIR as the controller. Assumes strength days are placed >=24 h before key bike sessions.

### New (no/recent lifting; **this athlete's starting category**)

| Phase | Sets x reps | Intensity | Frequency | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base wk 1-3 (anatomical adaptation) | 2 x 8-12 | 55-70% (RIR 3-4) | 2x | K (leg press/goblet), U (step-up light), H (trap-bar/RDL light), T, P; technique gates |
| Base wk 4-8 | 3 x 6-8 | 70-80% (RIR 2-3) | 2x | K, U, H, C, T, P; add single-leg hip flexion |
| Base wk 9-12 (max-strength intro) | 3 x 4-6 | 80-87% (RIR 1-3) | 2x | K (trap-bar/leg press), U, H, C |

| Build | 3 x 4-6 (main lift), 2 x 6-8 accessories | 82-88% (RIR 2) | 1-2x (2x only if bike load allows) | K, U, H, T; fast concentric; add on-bike torque/sprint work |
| Refine | 2-3 x 3-5 | 85-90% (RIR 2-3) | 1x | K, U + trunk; no new exercises |
| Taper (last 14 d) | 1-2 x 3-5 last session at ~10-14 d out; optional 2 x 3 @ 70% ~5-7 d out | 70-85% (RIR 3-4) | 0-1x | Neural primer only, no eccentric/novel work |

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Wk 1-2: 2-3 x 8-10; wk 3-8: 3 x 5-8; wk 9-12: 3 x 4-6 | 65% -> 85% (RIR 3 -> 1-2) | 2x | K, U, H, C, T, P |

### Experienced (>=2 y consistent heavy lifting)

| Phase | Sets x reps | Intensity | Frequency | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Hypertrophy/general 3 x 6-10 (3-4 wk), then max strength 3-4 x 3-5 | 75-85% then 85-92% (RIR 1-2) | 2-3x | K, U, H, C, hip flexion, T, P |
| Deload | 2 x 3-5 at held load | RIR 3-4 | 1x | Main lifts |
| Build | 3 x 3-5 | 85-90% (RIR 1-2) | 1-2x | K, H, U; add power (jump squat, loaded box jumps, or on-bike sprints) if intent and freshness allow |

**Cross-cutting rules for all rows:** rest 2-3 min between heavy sets; fast concentric; no failure sets; strength on easy or rest days; >=24 h before quality bike sessions; keep 1x/wk in Build/Refine if the athlete is coping (A/B); if the season macrocycle only allows 1x/wk overall, use one full session (3 x 4-6 heavy K/U/H + trunk) instead of two half sessions (B); if bike load is very high (>15 h/wk with a hard-week peak), fall back to 1x maintenance and note it to the athlete; for LBP/quad tightness flag: prefer trap bar/leg press over back squat and straight-bar deadlift in first two blocks.

## Open questions / low-confidence areas

1. **Ultra-specific effect (12+ h/day, multi-day).** No trial. The only durability data (Vikmoen 2017; Rønnestad 2011) are 3-h prolonged rides in well-trained cyclists (female cohort for the former). Confidence: very low for event-length claims.
2. **Steep-climb performance specifically.** No study directly tests strength on steep-climb power/low-cadence W/kg; the sprint/5-min results are the closest proxies. Whether on-bike low-cadence torque work is superior or additive is unresolved (only a 2025 on-bike-vs-squat trial, verified at snippet level).
3. **Meta-analytic certainty is LOW** (Llanos-Lagos 2025): small samples, unblinded, moderate risk of bias; frequency/duration moderators were not significant, so "2x vs 1x" during development phases has weak support beyond individual trials.
4. **Hinge/deadlift, hip thrust, trunk work**: absent from the classic cyclist protocols; recommendations are (B/C). Also a lack of cyclist-specific data on injury prevention effects of strength training.
5. **Strength standards for cyclists**: no verified source for relative-strength benchmarks (e.g., x BW); do not use as gates.
6. **Masters-specific volume/recovery**: few cycling RCTs; unclear whether 39 y needs lower volume. Louis 2012 and Bickel 2011 are indirect.
7. **Taper and strength**: no RCT on final-weeks strength timing; "stop ~10-14 d out / primer 5-7 d out" is convention (C).
8. **Same-day vs separate-day ordering**: conclusions rest on 15 mostly untrained-population studies (Vikestad & Dalen 2024); cyclist-specific evidence is inconsistent.
9. **Plyometric/power training for cyclists**: not systematically searched here; likely gap. 
10. **Unverified items flagged `[U]`:** Epley formula, Bosquet 2013 ~3-week retention timing, Spiering strength-maintenance specifics beyond the abstract summary, RAMP originals, Zourdos RIR validity, LBP prevalence details.
11. **PMC pages inaccessible (CAPTCHA):** PMC12244400 (on-bike vs off-bike), PMC12763815 (off-road gym vs on-bike), PMC11137631 (core vs conventional resistance) - details limited to search snippets; authors/journal not confirmed.
